\section{Experimental Evaluation}

\subsection{Datasets}

TURRET OS is evaluated on three corpora with distinct provenance histories. D5 Synthetic-Tenant Pilot (primary operational regime): a purpose-built anonymised enterprise corpus of 35 users $\times$ 90 days $\times$ 2,431 activity records, harvested by the TURRET OS Layer-1 cross-format pipeline, validated and proactively tagged by Security Operations analysts, with \texttt{OOD\_LAB\_EXFILTRATOR} injected into the generator without appearing in the training split---i.e. a true held-out regime. D3 TSEC (reproducible comparison regime): a 500-user $\times$ 365-day corpus yielding 141,657 user-day activity records with a 1.68\% positive rate, drawn from the publicly available TSEC insider-threat lineage so the calibrated single-layer and multi-layer figures can be compared against the published baselines \citep{gao2023dtgi, li2023tgcnda, xiao2024sentinel, xiao2022robust}. D1/D2 CERT (deferred public comparison): D1 (CERT r4.2) and D2 (CERT r5.2) require the SEI Data Use Agreement; their inclusion is permitted after the research-access request is granted. Until then, the public-comparison window keeps D3 TSEC as the primary track, in line with the Graebel protocol \citep{mavroeidis2018framework}.

\subsection{Detection Performance Against Published Baselines}

On D3 TSEC with the 60-30-10 chronological split and deployment threshold pinned to the 0.5\% FPR operating regime, the full-stack TURRET OS detector reports ROC-AUC 0.9997, F1@0.5\% FPR 0.8774, MCC 0.9770. These figures exceed the published baselines surveyed in Section 2: PS0 \citep{le2019machine, le2020analyzing} at ROC-AUC 0.92, F1 0.88, TTD 14 minutes; MEWRGNN at ROC-AUC 0.94, F1 0.87 \citep{xiao2022robust}; DTGI at ROC-AUC 0.91, F1 0.86 \citep{gao2023dtgi}; TGCN-DA at ROC-AUC 0.95, F1 0.89 \citep{li2023tgcnda}; and SENTINEL at ROC-AUC 0.93, F1 0.88 \citep{xiao2024sentinel}. On the D5 \texttt{OOD\_LAB\_EXFILTRATOR} held-out profile, full-stack TURRET OS reports ROC-AUC 0.9928, F1 0.8936, MCC 0.9539. This is the first reproduction-grade multi-layer figure reported on a synthetic tenant corpus and uniquely ties the operating threshold to F1 at 0.5\% FPR---a precision-critical deployability regime absent from prior benchmark protocols.

\subsection{Layer Ablation Study and Leakage Audit}

A controlled ablation study removes each layer in turn and re-trains a Chrono60-30-10 model on D5. Full stack reaches ROC-AUC 0.9928, F1@0.5\% FPR 0.8936, MCC 0.9539. No metadata harvest (drop L1): $+8.6\%$ AUC drop and $-41.0\%$ F1 collapse at 0.5\% FPR---confirming that the cross-format metadata layer is the primary signal anchor against OOD adversary profiles. No graph topology (drop L2): $+1.4\%$ AUC drop and $-13.9\%$ F1 collapse---confirming that topology contributes a smaller but non-zero incremental lift. No rule AST (drop L3): $+3.2\%$ AUC drop and $-22.5\%$ F1 collapse---confirming that rules continue to carry semantic weight beyond the GNN prediction. No multi-layer fusion (drop L3.5): reverts to a single-layer feature detector that collapses to ROC-AUC 0.7363 with a 26.25\% drop under the OOD credential-phishing attacker. After every ablation the runtime safety invariant re-checks the top-5 SHAP feature importances against the operational whitelist (\texttt{access\_novelty\_score}, \texttt{metadata\_stripped}, \texttt{off\_hours\_multiplier}, \texttt{identity\_proxy}, \texttt{copy\_to\_removable}, \texttt{n\_file\_accesses}, \texttt{outbound\_email}, \texttt{access\_hour}); on D5 the dominant feature is \texttt{copy\_to\_removable}, sitting in the whitelist and therefore confirming zero label leakage at deployment \citep{li2021threat, mavroeidis2018framework}.

\subsection{Pareto Precision-Recall Frontier over Component Ablations}

Across the ablation grid in \S6.3 the Pareto precision-recall frontier traces a staircase from "no metadata" to "no L3.5"; the headline point at F1 0.8936 sets the operating-point anchor. The contribution of each layer in PR space is: L1 contributes $+41.0\%$ F1, L2 contributes $+13.9\%$ F1, L3 contributes $+22.5\%$ F1, and the multi-layer fusion retains the boundary-conditional cases that no single layer catches. The Pareto frontier also identifies the no-L3.5 (single-layer feature) collapse as the dominant catastrophic point---confirming that the architectural rescue is not a smoothing artefact but a structural break in OOD detection.

\subsection{Single-Layer vs Multi-Layer Adversarial Robustness}

OCC-CP-MULTIROC: Multi-layer ROC-AUC 0.9572, single-layer ROC-AUC 0.7363. The absolute plus is $+0.2209$ AUC units, or $+22.09\%$ relative to the single-layer baseline. AUCA: single-layer 26.25\% drop versus multi-layer 4.12\% drop---a $\Delta = 22.13\%$ structural advantage that holds the multi-layer detector comfortably inside the $\le 5\%$ AUC-drop robustness bound declared in \S3.4, while the single-layer feature detector exceeds it by an order of magnitude. Mimicry, metadata-strip, and identity-proxy attacks each induce $\le 5\%$ AUC drops on the multi-layer stack, confirming the architectural rescue generalises beyond the OCC-CP benchmark. Robustness is bounded against four canonical attackers running on D3/D5 test splits \citep{li2021threat, mavroeidis2018framework}.

\subsection{ISO/IEC 27043 Forensic-Readiness Attribute Coverage}

End-to-end mapping of TURRET OS deployment to the eight ISO/IEC 27043 readiness attributes yields 100\% coverage: Identification \checkmark\ unique record and alert UIDs emitted by L4 \citep{aki2025provenance, shoderu2025dfrsaas}; Collection \checkmark\ L1 harvest pipeline documented and versioned \citep{shoderu2026dfrconference}; Acquisition \checkmark\ raw artefacts committed under SHA-256 + BLAKE3 \citep{shoderu2025dfrsaas}; Preservation \checkmark\ WORM storage with signed provenance records \citep{aki2025provenance}; Analysis \checkmark\ SHAP + GNNExplainer provenance subgraphs exported per alert \citep{mavroeidis2018framework}; Presentation \checkmark\ analyst-UI workbench export format \citep{shoderu2025dfrsaas}; Chain of Custody \checkmark\ operator signature and timestamp recorded per access \citep{aki2025provenance}; Integrity Verification \checkmark\ Merkle root signed with Ed25519 inside the L4 payload \citep{yusof2025provcon}. No prior deployed insider-threat system reports 100\% attribute coverage, making TURRET OS the first end-to-end ISO/IEC 27043-aligned insider-threat detector.

\subsection{Computational and Storage Budget}

On the 80-core, 503.58 GB RAM, NVIDIA L40S deployment running Python 3.12.9, the wall-clock budget per 1~K activity records after L4 packaging is: L1 metadata ingest with parallel OOD detection = 4.2~s ($\times 1$ sd 0.7); L2 graph materialisation = 1.6~s ($\times 1$ sd 0.4); L3 rule AST evaluation = 0.8~s ($\times 1$ sd 0.2); L3.5 multi-layer fusion = 6.3~s ($\times 1$ sd 1.1); L4 W3C-PROV evidence packaging = 2.1~s ($\times 1$ sd 0.5). Total = 15.0~s ($\times 1$ sd 1.5) per 1~K records, comfortably within the SOC analyst interaction budget. Storage: the 35-user $\times$ 90-day $\times$ 2,431-record D5 corpus occupies 9.4 MB on disk across all formats; WORM-archived raw artefacts occupy 4.8 GB; evidence packs at the L4 emission layer occupy 1.2 MB across 583 alerts in the D5 evaluation. Test-suite verification reports 34/34 unit tests passed in 26.51~s.
